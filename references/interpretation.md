# Durability interpretation framework

Use this reference only after checking session comparability. The patterns below generate hypotheses, not diagnoses.

## Pattern-to-hypothesis matrix

| Repeated pattern | Leading hypothesis | Important alternatives | Useful next comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heart rate rises while speed or power falls, especially in heat | Heat strain, hydration mismatch, over-fast pacing, or durability loss | Sensor drift, hills, wind, caffeine, illness | Similar route at cooler temperature or with a controlled opening |
| Heart rate stays stable or falls while output and form decline | Neuromuscular or structural fatigue | Low glycogen, pain inhibition, motivation, technical terrain | Same duration with strength block completed, gentler descents, or better fueling |
| Deterioration consistently improves with more carbohydrate | Fuel availability is an important limiter | Better pacing or cooler weather in the same sessions | Repeat the planned carbohydrate rate under similar conditions |
| Gastrointestinal tolerance fails before legs or heart rate | Gut tolerance, intake concentration, heat, intensity, or product choice | Illness, dehydration, unfamiliar food | Gut-training progression with one controlled product or concentration change |
| First-change time moves later but decoupling remains similar | Durability may be improving | Easier opening or better weather | Compare time-matched segments and RPE, not only whole-run percentage |
| Low decoupling but RPE and leg pain rise sharply | Cardiovascular metric is hiding mechanical cost | Route profile or heart-rate suppression | Examine cadence, power, descent load, pain, and next-day function |
| High decoupling with low RPE and a negative split | Warm-up, terrain, or calculation artifact may dominate | Sensor error or changing wind | Recalculate on stable middle segments |
| Recovery remains poor at 48 hours or disrupts training | Session dose or total load may be too high | Sleep, illness, travel, under-fueling | Reduce dose or improve recovery inputs before adding volume |
| Race fades but comparable training runs do not | Race pacing, taper, fueling, heat, or emotional intensity | Course specificity or accumulated race stress | Reconstruct the first third of the race and intake timeline |
| Same fade time across several well-matched runs | Stable duration-specific limitation is plausible | Habitual pacing or fueling schedule | Change one factor and observe whether onset moves |

## Interpreting decoupling

Treat the percentage as one summary of the heart-rate–output relationship, not the athlete's durability score.

- A lower value can coexist with substantial muscular, gastrointestinal, or cognitive deterioration.
- A higher value can result from heat, dehydration, altitude, hills, wind, stops, sensor problems, or an aggressive opening.
- An onset that moves later may be more meaningful than a small change in the final percentage.
- Three sessions can reveal a useful coaching pattern but do not establish a physiological cause.
- Do not use 5% as a universal pass/fail line across durations, sports, athletes, and environments.

## Choosing the primary investment

### Aerobic volume or effective duration

Prioritize when comparable sessions show repeatable late deterioration without strong evidence that fueling, heat, pacing, or pain explains it, and when current long-run exposure is clearly below event demands. Progress duration conservatively and preserve recovery.

### Strength and structural durability

Prioritize when leg output, form, cadence, impact tolerance, or downhill damage changes before cardiovascular strain. Choose maximum-strength, plyometric, eccentric, or terrain-specific work according to the event and athlete history; do not prescribe painful loading through injury.

### Fueling and gastrointestinal training

Prioritize when intake is low for duration and intensity, deterioration follows missed intake, performance improves with carbohydrate, or the gut becomes the first failed system. Change one variable at a time: total grams per hour, concentration, fluid pairing, product, timing, or food form.

### Heat adaptation and hydration planning

Prioritize when deterioration is substantially earlier or larger in hot or humid conditions. Separate heat-acclimation work from aggressive dehydration. Fluid needs are individual; do not prescribe replacement solely from population averages.

### Pacing

Prioritize when opening intensity is higher than planned, early RPE is already elevated, or race fades are not reproduced in controlled training. Use an intentionally restrained first segment as the discriminating test.

### Recovery or load reduction

Prioritize when baseline fatigue is high, recovery remains impaired at 48 hours, pain accumulates, or the field test disrupts ordinary training. More durability work is not useful if the athlete cannot absorb it.

### More measurement

Prioritize when sessions are poorly matched, important fields are missing, or several explanations remain equally plausible. Ask for the smallest additional dataset that can separate them.

## Confidence language

- **High confidence:** the pattern repeats in well-matched sessions and alternatives are weak.
- **Moderate confidence:** the pattern repeats, but one meaningful confounder remains.
- **Low confidence:** based on one session, unmatched conditions, missing data, or contradictory signals.

State what observation would lower or reverse the confidence.

## Four-week recommendation shape

Keep the follow-up experiment small:

1. Preserve most of the athlete's current training.
2. Change one primary variable tied to the leading hypothesis.
3. Repeat one comparable long run after sufficient adaptation time.
4. Record the same metrics and compare onset, magnitude, RPE, and recovery.
5. Escalate training only if the intervention is absorbed without persistent symptoms or recovery disruption.

For trail or ultra runners, the primary outcome may be later hiking transition, less speed loss after descents, sustained intake, fewer decision errors, or better feet and aid-station execution rather than lower whole-run decoupling.
